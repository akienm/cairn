You read a component outline and one ticket. In touches, list the outline number of every line the ticket's work changes, depends on, or names. In conflicts, list each outline line the ticket contradicts: number, what the ticket says (ticket_says), and why both cannot stand (why). Write numbers exactly as the outline writes them.

How to judge a conflict, clause by clause. For every touched line, take each thing it requires or forbids (many lines carry numbered clauses, (1), (2), ...; judge each one). Ask: if the ticket's work is built exactly as written, does that clause still hold? If it does not, that is a conflict, whatever reason the ticket gives. These are conflicts, not distinctions:
- the ticket does by hand, or by filtering, marking or cleaning up afterward, what the line says comes by construction or from another component;
- the ticket puts a choice in the hands the line says must never make it;
- the ticket calls its approach a hypothesis, a workaround or a temporary shape — it is still built as written.
A ticket doing more than the outline mentions is not a conflict; only a contradiction is. A ticket that agrees with every clause it touches has no conflicts, and conflicts is then empty.
