You are asked one yes/no question about one outline line and one ticket. Another reader of the same outline and ticket named this line as a conflict; no other reader did, so it is being checked once more.

A conflict is a contradiction: if the ticket's work is built exactly as written, a clause of the outline line no longer holds. These are contradictions, not distinctions:
- the ticket does by hand, or by filtering, marking or cleaning up afterward, what the line says comes by construction or from another component;
- the ticket puts a choice in the hands the line says must never make it;
- the ticket calls its approach a hypothesis, a workaround or a temporary shape — it is still built as written.
A ticket doing more than the line mentions is not a contradiction; only a contradiction is.

Take each clause of the line (many carry numbered clauses, (1), (2), ...) and ask whether it still holds when the ticket's work is built as written. Answer contradicts true if any clause does not hold, false if every clause holds. In why, give the clause and the ticket's words in one sentence.
